# Google Medical Ads Policy Check

Use this reference before writing, reviewing, or recommending Google Ads for healthcare, medical services, cosmetic clinics, dermatology, hair transplant, fertility, pharmacy, supplements, telemedicine, addiction, clinical trials, or any offer that mentions diagnosis, treatment, procedure, medicine, condition, recovery, pain, or patient outcomes.

## Official Sources To Check First

- Google Ads policy change log: https://support.google.com/adspolicy/topic/3230816
- Healthcare and medicines: https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031
- Restricted targeting in Personalized advertising: https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465
- Misrepresentation: https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6020955

Use the English policy text as the enforcement source. When internet access is available, verify the change log and the relevant policy pages before final copy, targeting, or landing page recommendations.

## Medical Policy Workflow

1. Record `Policy check date`, target country/city, landing page URL, and official sources checked.
2. Check whether the offer needs Google certification, local licensing, or country-specific restrictions.
3. Review ad copy, RSA assets, extensions, keywords, search terms, final URLs, landing page claims, testimonials, images, forms, and tracking.
4. Review audience usage. Treat health, medical, and cosmetic-treatment intent as sensitive. Be careful with Customer Match, Your Data segments, remarketing, lookalikes, audience expansion, custom segments, and creative or landing pages that imply sensitive health conditions.
5. Classify risk:
   - `Blocker`: prescription drug sales or online prescribing without required certification, unauthorized pharmacy claims, opioid promotion, unapproved substances, experimental/speculative treatment, clinical-trial claims that imply safety/effectiveness, fake qualifications, missing licenses, or deceptive medical capability.
   - `High`: cure/guarantee/permanent-result claims, unsafe superlatives, misleading before-after framing, pricing or offer details missing from the landing page, health-condition targeting, unsupported doctor/clinic superiority claims, or ad text that implies a user has a medical condition.
   - `Medium`: vague proof, weak disclaimers, missing privacy policy, unclear consultation process, mismatch between keyword intent and landing page, or medical claims not supported on-page.
   - `Low`: compliant service wording with clear provider identity, realistic CTA, visible location/contact/license signals, and no sensitive personalized targeting.
6. Produce safer wording when risk exists. Prefer service-availability and consultation language over outcome promises.

## Required Output Block

For every medical Google Ads audit, launch plan, or copy task, include:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Policy status | `Clear`, `Limited`, `High risk`, or `Blocked` |
| Date checked | Current date, or `not checked - no internet/tool access` |
| Sources checked | Official Google policy pages or local policy reference |
| Risky claims/terms | Exact ad text, keyword, landing page phrase, or audience setting |
| Safer replacement | Ready wording that avoids guarantees, diagnosis implications, or sensitive personalization |
| Targeting constraints | Audience, remarketing, custom segment, location, age, and data-use restrictions |
| Certification/location notes | Any healthcare certification, prescription, pharmacy, telemedicine, local law, or location uncertainty |
| Verification | What to recheck in Google Ads policy status, approvals, landing page, or change log |

## Safer Copy Patterns

- Use: "احجز استشارة لتقييم الحالة" instead of "حل نهائي مضمون".
- Use: "تحديد الخطة المناسبة بعد الكشف" instead of "أفضل نتيجة مضمونة".
- Use: "خدمات زراعة الشعر مع فريق طبي متخصص" instead of "استرجع شعرك بالكامل".
- Use: "تعرف على الخيارات المتاحة" instead of direct disease, pain, or diagnosis personalization.
- Avoid implying personal attributes such as "بتعاني من..." in ad copy when the targeting or creative can be interpreted as sensitive personalization.
